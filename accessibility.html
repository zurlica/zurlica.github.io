Accessibility Statement

The Zanzibar University Research Lab for Intelligent Computing and Analytics, ZURLICA, is committed to ensuring that our digital research environment, publications, postgraduate information, datasets, tools, and public communications are accessible to all users, including people with disabilities and users of assistive technologies.

Target standard: WCAG 2.2 Level AA • Last reviewed: 22 October 2026

Accessibility by design

Core accessibility features

ZURLICA applies inclusive design principles across the website. The following features are intended to support keyboard users, screen reader users, magnification users, neurodivergent users, and people with low vision, colour blindness, or motor differences.

Keyboard navigation

Main navigation, forms, filters, buttons, and interactive components are designed to be usable with a keyboard alone, including Tab, Shift + Tab, Enter, and Space.

Screen reader support

Semantic HTML landmarks, headings, lists, labels, ARIA attributes, and alternative text are used to improve compatibility with screen readers such as NVDA, JAWS, and VoiceOver.

Visible focus indicators

Interactive elements provide visible focus styling so keyboard users can identify where they are on the page.

Colour contrast and themes

The interface is designed to meet WCAG AA contrast expectations. Light and dark modes are available, and colour is not used as the only means of conveying information.

Zoom, reflow, and responsiveness

The layout adapts to different viewport sizes and supports zoom up to 200% without loss of content or functionality, where practical.

Accessible forms and feedback

Form fields use programmatic labels, error states are communicated clearly, and status messages are intended to be announced by assistive technologies.

Formal accessibility statement

Our commitment

ZURLICA believes that access to scientific knowledge, research opportunities, postgraduate training, datasets, publications, and analytical tools should be inclusive. We are committed to reducing digital barriers and continuously improving the accessibility of this website.

This statement applies to the public ZURLICA website hosted at https://zurlica.github.io. It covers core lab information, research descriptions, publications, team profiles, news, collaboration information, and contact channels.

Conformance status

This website is designed to conform to Level AA of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 , published by the W3C.

We do not claim perfect conformance across all present and future third-party content. Some legacy documents, embedded media, external services, or complex visualisations may only be partially accessible. Where limitations are identified, we aim to remediate them or provide an accessible alternative upon request.

Standards and references

ZURLICA aligns its accessibility work with recognised international references, including:

  • WCAG 2.2 at Level AA.
  • ISO/IEC 40500:2025, which corresponds to WCAG 2.2.
  • W3C ARIA specifications for accessible rich internet applications.
  • EN 301 549 accessibility requirements, where relevant to European partners, funders, or collaborative projects.

Inclusive design approach

ZURLICA applies a research-informed approach to digital accessibility. Our design process prioritises clarity, predictability, keyboard access, semantic structure, sufficient contrast, reduced reliance on colour, plain language, and compatibility with assistive technologies.

Where practical, we prefer accessible HTML, structured headings, descriptive links, text alternatives for meaningful images, captions or transcripts for multimedia, and downloadable data behind complex visualisations.

Accessibility of research outputs

As a research laboratory, ZURLICA recognises that accessibility extends beyond the website interface. We aim to make research outputs usable by scholars, students, practitioners, policymakers, and the wider public.

  • Publications: where possible, we provide accessible HTML, tagged PDFs, clear metadata, DOIs, preprints, and text-based summaries.
  • Figures and charts: meaningful visuals are accompanied by alternative text, data tables, captions, or downloadable source data.
  • Datasets: we prefer open, machine-readable formats such as CSV, JSON, or documented tabular files, with data dictionaries and licence information.
  • Code and software: repositories should include README files, installation instructions, usage examples, licence information, and issue-reporting guidance.
  • AI tools and models: where ZURLICA publishes or demonstrates AI systems, we aim to provide model cards, limitation statements, privacy notes, and human support channels where appropriate.
  • Dashboards and analytics: interactive dashboards should provide keyboard access, text summaries, and downloadable underlying data where feasible.
  • Events and seminars: we aim to provide accessible materials, captions or transcripts where practical, and advance notice of accessibility support.

Documents and downloadable content

ZURLICA prefers accessible document formats. Where PDFs are used, we aim to provide tagged PDFs or accessible HTML equivalents. Scanned documents, legacy reports, and third-party publications may not yet be fully accessible.

If you need an important ZURLICA document in an alternative accessible format, such as HTML, plain text, Word, large-print PDF, or another practical format, please contact us using the feedback channel below.

Third-party content and external links

Some pages may include content from third-party services, such as external journals, GitHub repositories, maps, social media embeds, video platforms, or partner websites. These services are outside ZURLICA’s direct control and may have their own accessibility standards and limitations.

When selecting third-party services, we aim to prefer providers with published accessibility statements and reasonable support for assistive technologies.

Known limitations

Despite our efforts, some parts of the website or related research materials may not yet fully meet WCAG 2.2 AA. Known or potential limitations include:

  • some legacy PDFs may not be fully tagged;
  • certain third-party embeds may have accessibility barriers;
  • complex interactive visualisations may require improvement;
  • some older images or figures may lack complete alternative text;
  • dynamic publication, team, or news filters depend on JavaScript;
  • some external publications may only be available in publisher-provided formats.

If you encounter a barrier that is not listed here, please report it. Your feedback helps us prioritise improvements.

Feedback, reporting barriers, and alternative formats

We welcome feedback on the accessibility of the ZURLICA website. If you experience difficulty accessing any content, service, publication, dataset, or tool, please contact us.

Email: zurlica@zanvarsity.ac.tz

To help us respond effectively, please include as much of the following as possible:

  • the web page address, or URL, where the problem occurred;
  • a clear description of the barrier you experienced;
  • the assistive technology you were using, for example screen reader, magnifier, voice control, or keyboard-only navigation;
  • your browser, device, and operating system, if known;
  • your preferred accessible format, if you are requesting one;
  • your contact details, if you would like a reply.

Response and remediation targets

ZURLICA aims to handle accessibility reports promptly and transparently. Our service targets are:

  • acknowledge feedback within 2 business days;
  • provide a substantive response or proposed solution within 10 business days;
  • for complex remediation or alternative format requests, provide an update within 20 business days;
  • prioritise barriers affecting postgraduate applicants, researchers, partners, and public users.

If you are not satisfied with our response, you may escalate the matter through the relevant Zanzibar University administrative or complaints channel. Please indicate in your email if you wish to escalate.

Assessment and testing methodology

ZURLICA reviews accessibility using a combination of automated checks, manual testing, and assistive technology validation. Our testing approach includes:

  • automated scans using tools such as axe-core and Lighthouse;
  • manual keyboard-only navigation testing;
  • screen reader checks using NVDA, JAWS, or VoiceOver where available;
  • zoom and reflow testing at 200% and narrow viewport sizes;
  • colour contrast analysis for text and interface components;
  • review of form labels, error messages, status announcements, and focus behaviour;
  • inspection of alternative text, headings, landmarks, tables, and lists;
  • review of reduced-motion behaviour for animated interface elements.

Accessibility testing is conducted during development and after significant site updates. However, automated tools cannot detect every barrier, so manual and user-centred review remains important.

Technology dependencies

The website works best with a modern browser and up-to-date assistive technologies. Some dynamic features, including publication filters, team profiles, and news listings, rely on JavaScript. If JavaScript is unavailable or disabled, core lab information and contact channels remain accessible, but some interactive directories may be limited.

If you cannot access a dynamic feature, please contact us and we will provide the requested information by email or another accessible format where practical.

Review of this statement

This accessibility statement was last reviewed on 22 October 2026. ZURLICA intends to review this statement at least annually and after major website, publication, dataset, or service changes.

We recognise that accessibility is a continuous process. As ZURLICA grows, we aim to strengthen accessible research communication across publications, data, software, dashboards, seminars, and postgraduate training materials.
